SST in miniature

The same content, two faces, one identity

Every piece of content on this page is an atom: a sentence-level unit whose identity is the SHA-256 hash of its normalized text.

Atoms compose into blocks, blocks into pages, and each level's identity is a Merkle root over its children — change one character anywhere and every fingerprint above it changes.

The page you are reading is Dual-Native: the human face is this rendered HTML; the machine face is a JSON-LD manifest in the head carrying the same atoms with their hashes.

The manifest is derived from the rendered page, never maintained by hand — the two faces cannot drift, because one is computed from the other at build time.

Anyone can verify this page without trusting its publisher: re-hash the visible text, recompute the Merkle roots, and compare against the manifest. The artefact carries its own proof.

A charter in the head declares the operator's terms: who may read, quote, ingest, or train on this content. Sovereignty is data, not a request.

Run the verifier against this very file to watch all six gates pass; flip a single character and watch them fail.

This entire implementation is one dependency-free script — the point of SST is not machinery, it is that identity, composition, and verification are simple enough to fit in it.

Beyond the content lies the geometry: a second Merkle root over every declared site — present or vacant — so the shape of what is missing is provable, not merely asserted.

This paragraph is one identity at two coordinates, printed twice on this page and listed once in the manifest: a bill of what the page carries, never a transcript of where.
